# Obsidian2Typst v0.15.2

**Release date:** 2026-11-16
**Minimum Obsidian version:** 1.5.0 (desktop only)
**Tests:** 2627 unit, plugin and tooling tests passed, 1 `todo`; 692/692 integration tests passing (working tree; the clean-clone gate of the release build is recorded in the project's state files)

The ◆ marks of a statblock's title band stand beside a name that wraps.

---

## What changes

- **The statblock band** (US-041, #170): the band was one text, `◆ name ◆`, so a
  creature name that wraps over several lines had the left ◆ at the start of
  its first line and the right ◆ at the end of its last line. Now:
  - a name that fits on one line with its marks is drawn exactly as before;
  - a name that wraps has a ◆ at the left end and a ◆ at the right end of the
    band, level with the middle of the name, which is centred and wraps between
    them;
  - a word that cannot fit beside the marks removes both marks (a word wider
    than the band is not broken, as before).
  The band's font, size and colour (`entries.statblock.fonts.band`,
  `sizes.band`, the accent) are used for the marks and the name; the gap is one
  space of that font.
- **Where it shows:** the PDF (the book and the statblock of `api.statblockTypst`,
  which Game Card Creator draws on its creature cards) and the Obsidian preview
  and the website export. A statblock whose name wrapped only because a mark
  wrapped alone is now lower (it can save one or two lines).
- The text of the Typst helper in the answer of `api.statblockTypst` changes
  (`prelude`); its shape, the data and `api.version` (1) do not.

## Limits

- In the preview (and on the website) CSS cannot tell whether a word fits beside
  the marks, so when a word is too wide there the marks wrap with the name
  instead of disappearing (decision D3 of the story). The preview's gap between a
  mark and the name is a quarter of an em, the PDF's is a space of the band font.
- Tested on: Obsidian 1.15.0 on macOS (Apple silicon), in a scratch vault with
  the sample preset (manual check M30, part A, at three band sizes). Not tested:
  Windows, Linux, mobile; the author's own vault and preset (part B of M30).

## Breaking changes and migration

None. A caller that compiles the answer's `prelude` gets the new helper with it;
Game Card Creator copies its fixture of the answer again.
